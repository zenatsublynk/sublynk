Fraud is organized, and increasingly transnational

Reported contractor fraud rose 38% from 2023 to 2025, costing homeowners and insurers an estimated $9B+ a year in post-disaster scams. Federal investigators have even identified an organized transnational crew posing as contractors to defraud homeowners with fake credentials.

Sublynk prevents this: verified license, active insurance, identity, and a background check are confirmed at onboarding, so fabricated or stolen credentials are flagged before a contractor ever joins a network.
Sources: NICB, NBC10 / DHS

Unlicensed operators are everywhere, and getting caught

Licensing boards run undercover sting operations that routinely catch a dozen or more unlicensed operators bidding real jobs in a single county. In California those citations now carry administrative fines up to $15,000 plus misdemeanor charges, and homeowners who hire them have little legal recourse.

Sublynk prevents this: license validation gates entry to a network. Only contractors with a verified, active license in the right trade and state get in.
Source: CSLB sting operations (FOX 11)

Storm chasers turn disasters into a roofing racket

Roofing fraud costs US homeowners an estimated ~$3 billion a year. After every major storm, unlicensed "storm chasers" flood the area, collect deposits or insurance checks, and disappear, often leaving a torn-apart roof behind.

Sublynk prevents this: networks can gate disaster and insurance work to verified, insured contractors only, and homeowners hiring from a Sublynk Certified profile know exactly who they are letting on the roof.
Source: NICB

EPA lead-safe penalties are now $46,989 a day

As of January 12, 2026, the EPA can assess up to $46,989 per violation, per day when an uncertified renovator works on pre-1978 housing without proper lead-safe (RRP) practices. Missing certifications, poor dust containment, and false records are the common failures.

Sublynk prevents this: RRP and trade certifications are tracked with renewal dates, so a network can gate remodeling work to contractors who are currently certified, not expired.
Sources: US EPA, Florida Realtors
⚖️

Licensing enforcement is escalating fast

California's SB 779 raised the minimum fine for unlicensed work from $200 to $1,500 (a 650% jump) effective July 1, 2026, lifted workers-comp violation fines to $10,000 to $20,000, and empowered the Attorney General to pursue contractors with unpaid wage judgments. At least four states, including a full overhaul in New Jersey, tightened licensing in 2026.

Sublynk prevents this: credentials and compliance status are tracked automatically and kept current across every state a network operates in, so tightening rules never turn into surprise penalties.
Sources: Bloomberg Law, CSLB
☣️

Hazmat work carries seven-figure liability

More than $30 billion sits in asbestos bankruptcy trusts, a stark measure of the liability exposure when unqualified crews disturb hazardous materials. One uncertified crew on the wrong job can create a claim that outlives the project by decades.

Sublynk prevents this: abatement, mitigation, and hazmat certifications are verified, so networks can confirm only qualified, certified crews touch dangerous work.
Source: Asbestos trust fund reporting

The vetting gap under a labor shortage

Over 80% of contractors report they cannot find enough qualified workers and subs. Under that pressure, vetting slips: crews get hired on familiarity instead of fit, and prequalification data ages quickly as insurance and certifications lapse between jobs.

Sublynk prevents this: a pre-vetted pool with automatic annual renewal of insurance and credentials means a network can staff from contractors whose vetting is always current, without the manual chase.
Source: Associated General Contractors of America
